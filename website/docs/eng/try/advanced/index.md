# Advanced use

When you delegate complex work, it helps to understand the system you are forming, how decisions connect, and what supports the result's fitness for use. Develop these skills through your own tasks; knowing iEWR's internals is not a prerequisite for starting.

Here, an engineer is someone who deliberately creates or changes systems and assesses results within their responsibility. A system may be an application, a product, a document, an organizational structure, or a working arrangement.

## Work with the system as a whole

The basis is [System Formation](/iEWR/eng/foundations/system-formation/): the next work follows from the system needed, what already exists, and what is missing for its use.

A complaint about a request form, for example, may point to an interface defect, a processing rule, or a misunderstanding of the form's purpose. Ask the agent to investigate the cause and its relation to the whole system so it can identify the necessary change.

The agent needs material information about purpose, surroundings, current decisions, state, and dependencies. It recovers and organizes the engineering context; you provide available facts and report new decisions. The entire project need not remain in the chat's memory.

## Practical engineering skills

- **Explain the purpose.** What are we creating or changing, who needs it, and how will it be used? If the answer is unclear, describe the problem.
- **Provide the grounds.** Supply materials, current decisions, and constraints. Ask the agent to establish the current state, confirmed properties, and unknowns.
- **Delegate the result.** Set the expected outcome and bounds of independence. The agent determines the necessary work and methods for the task.
- **Make significant decisions.** Discuss alternatives, consequences, and grounds. Give permissions within your authority.
- **Assess fitness for use.** Compare the result with its purpose, performed checks, and conditions of use. A specific discrepancy becomes an assignment for revision.
- **Continue and reconsider.** Report new facts and requirements. After a break, recover current decisions, changes, and open questions with the agent.

## Assign a complex result

Describe the whole, the desired properties, and significant boundaries. You do not need to list every operation in advance.

> We need a working arrangement for transferring product support to another team. Investigate the current situation, compare options, and prepare a coherent set of roles, access, knowledge, interactions, and readiness checks. Carry out preparation and analysis independently. Bring decisions about participants, authority, and changes to live systems back to me. Show what is still missing for the transition.

This mandate connects the result, permitted actions, and human decisions. You can think through the question together or delegate an outcome within agreed bounds; choose the form of participation for the task.

## The Engineering Team leader's own work

A leader needs the same skills, plus the ability to hold together a complex or uncertain system:

- Clarify intent, use, and fitness criteria while the goal is still forming.
- Assign connected engineering contributions, investigate hypotheses about needs and value, and compare significant alternatives by benefit, full cost, and consequences.
- Check decisions against architecture, surroundings, dependencies, and confirmed properties. Identify missing knowledge, resources, tests, permissions, or another participant's result.
- Reconsider goals and decisions when circumstances change, retaining results whose grounds remain valid.
- Check how the parts work together and what value arises in actual use. Completed development, successful checks, and benefit to the user are distinct results.
- Judge sufficiency: finish when the result has been obtained, checked to the necessary extent, and obligations fulfilled. An old task queue may need revision.

## When a team is involved

Shared context helps reveal connections, but joint work also needs organization: agreed goals, contributions, responsibility, authority, dependencies, handoffs, and integration of results.

Designing the Engineering Team itself is a separate engineering task with the relevant participants and mandate. Retained project files support it; they do not automatically create agreements.

## Assess your progress

Take a real task through to a useful result. Can you explain its purpose, what supports its fitness, and what remains unknown? Can you resolve discrepancies and change direction when circumstances change while retaining AI's independence?

That ability matters more than completed task counts or terminology. [Start working](/iEWR/eng/try/) with an ordinary conversation; [How iEWR works](/iEWR/eng/how-iewr-works/) is available whenever you want to explore further.
