# FPF, Dynamic Method Generation, and MethodPack

Engineering work needs a suitable way of obtaining the result. In iEWR, **FPF supplies general methodology, DMG obtains the method needed for the task, and MethodPack preserves the applied repertoire**.

## What FPF contributes

First Principles Framework (FPF), by Anatoly Levenchuk and contributors, provides concepts, distinctions, principles, and methods for engineering reasoning across domains. It makes important relationships explicit so they can be applied, checked, and reconsidered.

“The architecture is ready” may refer to a diagram, an accepted decision, implemented components, or a checked system. “The check passed” may refer to one test rather than every property required for use. “The agent can change the server” may describe technical access rather than permission.

FPF helps distinguish a system from its description, a method from performed work, a plan from execution, a result from evidence about its properties, and capability from authority. These distinctions affect what should happen next.

Its practical contributions include maintaining coherent meaning, grounding conclusions, and selecting methods. A capable model already has substantial domain knowledge. FPF provides an explicit methodological basis for organizing its use.

## Choose a method for the actual difficulty

Suppose sales have fallen. More advertising, different prices, a product change, and a delivery investigation could all be proposed. Familiarity with advertising does not establish that it addresses the cause.

First recover what changed, what is known, which assumptions matter, and what result is missing. Then select a way of obtaining the needed knowledge or changing the situation. A useful method is connected to the difficulty and the conditions for resolving it.

A method may come from FPF, a professional standard, a paper, a book, a domain framework, or accumulated experience. It must contain enough of the actual way of working: inputs, operations, conditions, significant connections, and ways to check or stop.

## DMG obtains the way of working

**Dynamic Method Generation (DMG)** covers search, reuse, adaptation, and construction. Generation includes obtaining a suitable existing method; it does not mean inventing one for every task.

For a new choice, the agent first looks for a sufficient contribution in FPF and reads the relevant original with its conditions. Where domain detail or local adaptation is needed, it consults MethodPack. It supplies a remaining gap using professional knowledge, available sources, and experience.

A sufficient basis already read is reused while its conditions hold. The process does not require traversing all FPF or reopening the repertoire before every action. Missing essential knowledge is identified specifically rather than filled by a plausible-sounding method name.

New observations can change the required result or method. Finding a technique, explaining it, applying it, and establishing its usefulness remain different achievements.

## MethodPack preserves a usable repertoire

**MethodPack (MP)** is one applied repertoire for the project: a thematic book of descriptions and an index leading to exact bodies and editions. It includes environment support, general-purpose methods, and specialization accumulated during work.

A reusable description records the result and conditions of use, inputs and means, significant operations and branches, checking and stop conditions, sources and editions, relationships, experience, and limitations relevant to use.

A one-off action can finish with its result. A contribution useful again can be retained. When a used description changes materially, previous editions and the relevant checking grounds are preserved. The connected book and index are checked before a new pair is activated.

Project facts, individual assignments, and execution logs remain working materials rather than automatically becoming methods.

## Methods, DPFs, skills, and files

A **method** is a way of doing work. A **Domain Principles Framework (DPF)** organizes related domain principles and methods. A **skill** or file can deliver method content and instructions to an agent. Actual application is the work performed with those means.

These forms can cooperate. A skill may contain a complete method, and a DPF may be delivered through a skill. The contribution is judged by what it enables for the task, not the carrier's name.

## Checking and practical value

Availability does not establish successful application. Check the result against the assigned use and retain observations with the method edition and operating conditions. A changed source or condition can reopen the part that depends on it.

Methodology also consumes effort: finding and interpreting sources, preserving distinctions, and checking outcomes. For a simple reversible action, a direct solution may suffice. Comparative value is established on tasks with attention to fitness, significant errors, rework, and total costs.

People retain goals, value judgments, and undelegated decisions. Methods help organize the work; their presence does not supply missing data, tools, or authority.
