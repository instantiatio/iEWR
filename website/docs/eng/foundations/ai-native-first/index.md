# Nine AI-native-first principles

*Principles edition: 1.1.*

**AI-native-first** is an approach to designing activity around the capabilities of artificial intelligence. It reconsiders which functions are necessary, who should perform them, and how to obtain the result.

The approach combines independent intellectual execution by AI, checking results, and human control over goals, authority, and consequential decisions. The nine principles describe complementary aspects of this organization of activity; they do not rank the importance of its functions.

## Principles and intellectual roots

| Principle | Intellectual roots | Meaning in AI-native-first work |
|---|---|---|
| Teleology | Aristotle; philosophy of purpose | The goal determines the organization of activity |
| Axiology | Philosophy of values | Evaluation and choice guide the creation and realization of alternatives |
| Human agency | Philosophy of action and autonomy | People retain the right to determine goals, delegation bounds, and decisions |
| Presumption of AI execution | AI-native approaches; engineering of autonomous systems | Consider AI as the default performer of intellectual work |
| Functionalism | Functional analysis; systems engineering | Preserve necessary functions rather than familiar procedures and roles |
| Subsidiarity | Political and social philosophy; control theory | Delegate work and decisions to the lowest sufficient level |
| Verifiability | Epistemology; philosophy of science | Establish fitness through checks and evidence appropriate to the intended use |
| Adaptivity | Cybernetics; control theory | Feedback changes work, methods, and, when necessary, criteria and goals |
| Cognitive ergonomics | Cognitive science; engineering psychology | Preserve human attention for decisions that need it |

These roots identify relationships between ideas, not equivalence to the named theories. “Presumption of AI execution” is an author-defined formulation.

## I. Intent and choice

### 1. Teleology: the goal determines the organization of activity

Start with the desired result, its purpose, criteria of achievement, and constraints. These determine the necessary work, its composition, and ways of performing it. Existing processes, roles, and tools do not by themselves justify continuing an activity.

Choose ways of reaching the goal with regard to result quality, consequences, and total costs. New knowledge may require refinement of the goal and the conditions for achieving it.

### 2. Axiology: evaluation and choice guide the creation of alternatives

Creating alternatives and deciding which deserve realization are different functions. Creation expands the possible solutions. Evaluation relates them to the result's value, criteria, constraints, and consequences.

When AI reduces the cost of generating alternatives, their number may grow. Quantity does not determine which are needed. Evaluation and choice guide further inquiry, development, and realization; they do not replace or diminish that work.

AI can investigate alternatives, assess consequences, and help reconsider criteria. Consequential choices remain with whoever holds the corresponding authority.

### 3. Human agency: people determine goals and bear responsibility

People retain the right to determine and revise goals, choice criteria, and delegation bounds. AI may propose goals, alternatives, and assessments. Reasoning ability does not confer authority to change an assignment or its acceptable risk.

Responsibility requires access to the grounds of consequential decisions, necessary information, and the ability to intervene within one's authority. A person need not perform every operation or check personally.

**The ability to perform work and the right to determine its goals are distinct.**

## II. Organization of activity

### 4. Presumption of AI execution: AI as the default performer

When designing intellectual activity, consider execution by AI first. Assign work to it when its capabilities and means are sufficient for the required quality, costs and risks are acceptable, and the necessary actions are permitted.

People may choose collaboration, perform particular actions themselves, or involve a specialist for a substantive reason. Functions are allocated according to the task and execution conditions rather than a familiar structure of job titles.

**AI is the default performer of intellectual work when the necessary conditions are provided.**

### 5. Functionalism: preserve functions rather than familiar ways of performing them

When changing the organization of activity, preserve functions needed for the result. Previous roles, stages, documents, and approvals may change. Mandatory constraints, checking, and responsibility remain in force.

Select methods and work according to the situation. Reuse an adequate existing result. Remove actions that add nothing to the result or obligations. Work ends when the assigned result is sufficient and the relevant obligations are fulfilled.

This also applies to AI environments. If a function is already adequately provided by the model, a domain method, or an existing tool, a new software mechanism is not needed for its own sake.

**Preserve necessary functions, not historically established ways of performing them.**

### 6. Subsidiarity: independence at a sufficient level

Delegate work and decisions to a performer capable of obtaining the required result within the bounds of authority, costs, and risks. Intervention by a higher level needs a reason rather than serving to confirm every operation.

AI can choose methods, make plans, and act within the assignment. People participate where their competence, authority, or an undelegated decision is needed. Software provides technical limits that cannot reliably be maintained by an instruction to the agent alone.

While one part awaits a person's decision, independent authorized work can continue.

**Independence is determined by capability, authority, and consequences, rather than a desire to exclude people.**

## III. Environment and feedback

### 7. Verifiability: results need appropriate checking

A persuasive explanation, a coherent model, or a successful operation does not by itself establish fitness for use. Checks must establish the properties on which the intended use relies.

The depth and independence of checking depend on the task and the consequences of error. Repeated work may need statistical quality controls. Critical constraints and irreversible actions need stronger guarantees, with technical measures selected for the actual conditions and consequences.

AI can perform checks but must not arbitrarily change its own evaluation criteria. Checking grounds and the right to accept a result must match the assignment and current authority.

**Trust in a performer does not replace evidence of result fitness.**

### 8. Adaptivity: feedback changes activity

Intent, action, observation, and correction form a loop of interaction with reality. Compare the result with the required properties and constraints. Discrepancies provide grounds for changing further work.

The response may require a new check, a different method, revised actions, reduced independence, or a stop to dependent work. Significant new information may justify changes to criteria and goals by the person or performer authorized to make them.

Feedback helps detect the consequences of errors and prevent uncontrolled continuation on faulty grounds. It does not guarantee error-free execution.

**Results and new information change subsequent work and, when necessary, its grounds.**

### 9. Cognitive ergonomics: attention as a limited resource

Human attention is limited. As AI capabilities expand, attention may constrain the selection of directions, evaluation of results, and consequential decisions.

The environment should help recover context, organize authorized work, and present the information needed for a person's decision. It need not display every technical operation or continually involve the person in the performer's internal organization.

Minimizing questions at any cost is not the aim. Preserve human participation where it matters to result value, authority, risk, and responsibility.

**Preserve human attention for the decisions that need it.**

## Working formulation

The goal determines the organization of activity. Evaluation and choice guide creation and realization. AI is considered the default intellectual performer, while people retain control over goals, delegation, and consequential decisions. Results are checked; feedback changes the work. The environment supports this activity without spending human attention unnecessarily.

The fitness of a particular arrangement depends on result quality, acceptable consequences, and the full cost of obtaining the result. The general principle is **the greatest justified independence of the performer, with the necessary control over results and consequences**.
