# Creating and developing a DPF

Use this entry when Human has assigned obtaining a Domain Principle Framework (DPF) or substantively revising an existing one. A DPF gives its user a connected system of patterns for recurring domain problems: how to recognize the situation, choose a useful action, obtain a result, and see the limits of use. No special start command is needed; the instruction determines the desired result and Human participation.

This is navigation to the original method, not a local replacement. An ordinary description of a project method and an entry in the [method repertoire](PROJECT_METHODS.md) can remain useful results in their own right. A file, YAML entry, successful trial, or several pattern-like headings do not themselves make a result a DPF.

## First useful result

Under E.4.DPF, start with the reader, recurring problem, first useful action and result, and ordinary stop or return when the course is wrong. Recover needed contributions and their relations from the task and sources; then compare them with exact existing contributions from FPF, supplied DPFs, and domain sources. A topic name or missing index entry does not establish a gap.

Check whether direct source use, a short guide, a contribution to an existing framework, or more convenient access is sufficient. The canonical method compares five outcomes: a new or revised framework, contribution to an existing one, a result of another kind, a thinner publication or access path, or no new maintained product now. These are alternatives, not sequential stages.

If a narrow result fully answers the question and leaves no architectural boundary used later, work may stop there without a decision record. If further work depends on field selection, edition, dependencies, pattern placement, material relations, or publication, use **E.4.PFAD** for an answer in one **E.9 DRR**. Keep the answer, decision record, and acceptance of the answer distinguishable. Obtain a missing material Human decision before a dependent action; an already delegated choice needs no repeated permission.

Before authoring a new edition, check the scope of the promise under E.4.DPF: connected problem families, coverage, material pattern relations, representative application, the complete set for first use, external dependencies, honest omissions, and revision conditions. One useful pattern is a reason to check scope; pattern count alone does not determine whether a framework has been obtained.

## When a DPF is selected

Follow the applicable original route, reading dependency bodies when their conditions arise:

- Select sufficient source work. One exact source may suffice; **F.0.2** is needed for the corresponding meaning comparison, and **G.2** for a broad maintained SoTA pack when the task needs one. Preserve borrowed and rejected contributions, editions, limits, and update conditions.
- Create patterns through **E.8**. The first substantive seed already follows its canonical form; maturity requires usable actions, explanations, cases, boundaries, and sources. **F.18** determines stable names; assert material relations explicitly; add **E.4.PFR** records for necessary maintenance uses.
- Check individual patterns through **E.21**, the package through **E.4.DPF.DA**, and publication form through applicable **E.11.PFP** and other necessary original conditions. **E.19** applies to claimed admission, **E.23** to the corresponding improvement, and **G.11** to currency review. An author's preliminary check does not replace the independent complete E.21 required for a material maturity or readiness conclusion.
- Preserve the exact edition, grounds, assessment results, and their limits. Domain claims, examples, source returns, and boundaries remain in the user's DPF; development and check materials are stored separately when their later use requires it.

This list helps locate the route. Performance and sufficiency are determined by the original's full applicable conditions, not by the presence of references or check marks in a list.

## Completion and handoff

Compare the assigned result with the actual one. Deliver a seed as a seed; it does not replace an assigned ready DPF. A ready DPF requires usable pattern bodies, grounds for their quality, package assessment for the exact use, and fulfilled applicable publication, admission, and currency conditions. E.4.DPF.DA requires a grounded result for all D1–D12 and applicable form checks; missing evidence remains a gap.

Connect the material requirement, exact result edition, observation or evidence, and conclusion. Independent checking means appropriate grounds and checking conditions; a new context or a different model name does not itself establish independence. Successful assembly, filled sections, and a single trial do not replace suitability checking.

Preserve the original edition and provenance of a used DPF, source terms, and further-use conditions. An update does not silently switch previous uses. Creating a DPF does not authorize changing the original FPF, external publication, or deployment; these require their own grounds under [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).
